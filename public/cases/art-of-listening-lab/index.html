The Art of Listening

An offline music laboratory. This prototype proves the hard part first: every change below rewrites the notes or the timing, then plays them. Nothing is a fixed answer.

ReadySaved on this device

Download WAV renders the built-in notes. Record a take plays your music once and saves what you hear, which also works for real recordings. Downloads work best in the ZIP version opened on your computer. This online preview may block them.

This is a real recording, so its notes cannot be rewritten. Instruments and Key and mode do nothing here. Dynamics, Tempo and the nature sound still work, and the pitch stays the same when you change the speed.

Für Elise is built in: public-domain notes played by a synthesizer (simulated, not a real recording). Your own recordings stay in this browser.

1. Discover the orchestra

Pick the lead instrument. Levels are matched so you hear timbre, not volume.

Timbres here are synthesized. Real instrument recordings and stems arrive when licensed audio is bundled.

Why can the same melody sound like a different character speaking when the instrument changes?

2. The same tune, a different world

Major and Dorian change the actual notes (C, F, G are raised), they are not a pitch shift. Transposing keeps every interval. Watch the staff and its ♯ signs change.

Does a melody's character live in its notes alone, or also in its register, harmony and the listener?

3. The shape of emotion

Drag the dots to set how loud the music is. Use the buttons to add or remove dots (double-click works too).

Keyboard: click the curve, PageUp/PageDown picks a point, arrow keys move it, Enter adds, Delete removes. Terms: pp very soft, p soft, mp medium-soft, mf medium-loud, f loud, ff very loud, crescendo gets louder, diminuendo gets softer, sforzando (sfz) one sudden strong note (try a tall sharp point).

Does the most important moment need to be the loudest?

4. Time has a personality

Same editor, now for speed. Pitch stays put because only the timing changes.

One steady speed:

Largo very slow. Adagio slow and calm. Andante a relaxed walking pace. Moderato medium. Allegro quick and lively. Presto very fast. Accelerando gets faster, ritardando gets slower, rubato bends the time freely like a rubber band. Same keyboard keys as the volume curve.

Is the music rushing toward something, enjoying the moment, or holding something back?

7. Teach your AI what you mean

Tell it what to change. Try: "Make the ending quieter and slower", "I like the flute but it's too cheerful", "Keep the melody clear".
